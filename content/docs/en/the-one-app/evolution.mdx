A Space can get better at its own work. Two loops do this, both from evidence of real use: the **nightly review**, which runs by itself each night, looks at where Agents kept struggling and makes small changes to memory and Skills; and the **evolution matter**, opened only when you ask, where a lab of three Agents tests one change at a time to your teams, roles, Skills or the main assistant.

Neither loop asks for approval. Every change is recorded, measured afterwards against what it was meant to fix, and can be undone. They change only a Space of your own; the official Space is out of their reach (see the last section). For the idea behind this, see [Self-Evolution](../multi-agent-architecture/self-evolution).

## The nightly review

Every Space has a built-in trigger, **Nightly review**, at 03:30 this computer's time. Before anything starts, the app checks whether there is something to review: new friction since the last review, an earlier change past its deadline without effect, or an alert on one. If there is nothing, it records a skip with the reason and spends nothing. It does not keep the computer awake; if the computer sleeps through 03:30, the review runs once the computer wakes.

Its trigger is in **Settings › Runs by itself**, marked **Built in**: switch it off, change its time, rename it or delete it, and the app does not put it back. See [Triggers and running in the background](./triggers).

### Friction

Friction is where Agents kept struggling in this Space's real use. The app finds it in its records without calling a model, in seven kinds: **Repeated failure**, **Loop**, **Detour**, **Asked you**, **Rework**, **Kept erroring** and **Colleague rework**.

### What the Reviewer does

When the review runs, an Agent called the Reviewer:

1. looks first at its earlier changes, the worst reading first, and undoes or narrows those that made things worse;
2. picks the few new findings that recur and makes the smallest lasting change for each: a lesson in the Space's memory, or an edit to one of its Skills, at most three changes a night; when unsure, it first tries the change on the step where the friction happened;
3. writes a fix proposal instead when the cause is out of its reach, such as a tool, a role's prompt, a team, or something you must decide;
4. registers each change beforehand: which friction it should reduce, which must not rise, by how much, and by when (28 days unless it says otherwise).

Its changes take effect at once, without anyone's approval, and go into the action log like any Agent's edits (see [Action log, undo and stopping](./action-log)). The review puts nothing in the strip of what waits for you and notifies no one; you read what it did in the weekly report.

### Reading and undoing it in the weekly report

**What it changed by itself**, the first section of the [Weekly report](./weekly), covers the reviews of the last 7 days. Its top line says how many changes helped and stayed, then how many reviews ran, how many changes they made, how many you undid, how many got worse, how many passed their deadline, and what it all cost. Below it, a line gives the Reviewer's record over 90 days: of the changes with a verdict, how many helped. A week without a review says only **No review ran in these 7 days. Each night it first checks the records, and starts only when they show a new place where work keeps getting stuck.**

Each change of the week follows, the ones that got worse first, then the ones past their deadline. Each change shows:

- what it changed (**Memory**, **Skill**…), which item, whether it was created or modified and when, and what the Reviewer said beforehand it expects;
- its readings: for each friction it answers, how often that friction happened per 100 chances before the change and since, for example "3.1 → 0.4 per 100 calls"; with too few chances so far it says **Too early** and how many more it needs;
- **Guard** readings: frictions the change must not make worse, and the first-pass rate, rework, cost and time to first delivery of the team where the friction happened, read the same way;
- a status: **Better**, **Worse**, **No change yet**, **Too early**, **Past its deadline without effect**, **Still flat after two weeks** or **Undone**.

Some changes also show:

- **Tried on the step**: the Reviewer tried the change on the step where things went wrong before making it, and the line says how often it came out right as it was and with the change;
- an **Alert**: the friction it answers keeps coming as often as before, so the Reviewer looks at it first;
- "with nothing changed, M of N readings here moved as much": the change in the reading is within how much this computer's readings move by themselves, so it counts as neither better nor worse.

**Show the change** shows the diff. **Undo** takes the change back through the action log, along with other files the same call changed; an undone change can be brought back with **Restore**.

Last comes a line for each night: when the review ran, how many frictions it found and how many of them happened again, how many changes it made and what it cost; below it, the **Fix proposals** and **Task drafts** the Reviewer wrote in these 7 days.

Readings are evidence for the Reviewer and for you; the app never undoes a change on its own. A change you undo is your verdict, and the Reviewer's procedure tells it not to make the same change again.

## The evolution matter

The evolution matter opens only when you ask for it. Tell the main assistant something like "make this Space better at its own work" or "improve how the software team checks its work". It then:

1. opens the evolution matter, titled "Evolution", or reopens it if the Space already has one: there is one per Space;
2. gives the first census to the lab, with a folder for the lab's files (`evolution/`, unless you name another);
3. gives the matter a weekly cadence, a trigger named "Weekly census" that fires **Every Monday at 10:00, only when there is new friction**, so a quiet week costs nothing;
4. tells you in a sentence or two what the matter does and when it runs.

It is not opened for every Space by itself, because a standing cadence spends model calls.

### How the lab works

The lab is a working group of three Agents, named **Self-improvement lab** on boards and in Teams and tools: the **Director** runs the census and decides, the **Patcher** makes the change, and the **Verifier** checks it. Each round, started by the weekly census or by a request from you or the main assistant, goes like this:

1. **Census.** The Director reads what went wrong in real use since the last census, including how the nightly review's changes are doing, and sorts the failures by mechanism.
2. **At most one hypothesis.** It picks only something that recurs and has a clear mechanism, or nothing. Before the first reading it writes down the target cases, what should change and by how much, what must not get worse, the bar for shipping, and a deadline.
3. **The smallest layer.** The Patcher changes the smallest thing that failed: a memory, a Skill, a role, a team or code.
4. **Cheapest checks first.** The Verifier replays the step that went wrong, then runs the target cases beside the current version in the same batch, then cases the change was not tuned on, and last a set of sealed cases the lab never tunes on.
5. **Decide.** The Director ships the change only when it clears the bar written down beforehand, with the old version frozen as a copy so the change can be taken back. Otherwise the idea is abandoned and its evidence kept.
6. **Scorecard.** Every change goes into the lab's scorecard, with its hypothesis, evidence, verdict and cost, and afterwards whether it was kept, undone or made things worse.

The lab works like any working group: you can talk to it, pause it or look at it in the Theater. See [Working groups](./working-groups).

### The lab on the matter's page

The evolution matter's page shows the lab above the board, under **Evolution · lab**:

- four figures: **Changes shipped** and how many were kept, **Changes tried**, **Spent on trials**, and **Next census**;
- **Under test now**: this round's change, the layer it touches, when it started, its readings so far and what the group is doing;
- **Changes tried**, newest first: the date, what was tried and its readings, the verdict (**Testing**, **Shipped** or **Abandoned**; once shipped, **reading after ship** and then **kept**, **undone** or **regressed**) and the cost;
- **The lab's rules** and **What a change goes through**, the steps above;
- **Change its direction**: write what the lab should improve first or leave alone, and press **Tell the main assistant**. It passes your words on, and its answer appears on the home page.

## The official Space is out of reach

The official Space is read-only: its working-group templates, roles and Skills are kept by The One and updated with new versions. Neither loop can change it: the nightly review's changes to it are refused, and the lab keeps what it finds as proposals, with their evidence, and asks you once whether to copy the Space.

Copy the official Space and use the copy, and their changes take effect: Get started offers **Copy the official Space and use it**, and [Definition Spaces](./capabilities/definition-spaces) lets you do it any time. The cost is that later updates to the official Space no longer reach your copy by themselves.
