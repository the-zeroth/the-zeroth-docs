**Settings** is where you decide how The One runs on your computer: your account and updates, how the app looks, the connections to model services and which model Agents use by default, what keeps running in the background, your phone and other programs, what Agents changed and how much disk the app takes, and what developers look at. Open it from the bottom of the sidebar; it starts on **General**.

What the work is done with, such as the working groups, roles, Skills and Plugins, lives in [Teams and tools](../capabilities) instead.

Settings has six sections. Each opens on its first page, and a section with more than one page names its pages above the page, for example **General** and **Appearance**.

| Section | Its pages |
| --- | --- |
| **General** | **General**, **Appearance** |
| **Models and sign-in** | **Model connections**, **Default model** |
| **Runs by itself** | **Background & triggers** |
| **Phone and other programs** | **Phone channel**, **Outside callers** |
| **Data and undo** | **Action log**, **Storage**, **Memory recall** |
| **Developer** | **Developer view**, **Evaluations** |

## General

### General

- **Account**: the email you are signed in with, whether it is a personal or an organization account, and **Log Out**.
- **Language**: the **Interface language**, English or 简体中文. Your choice is kept on this computer and used from then on instead of the system language.
- **Software updates**: the version you run and its release date. The app checks for updates after it starts and downloads a new version in the background, with its progress in the title bar. **Check Updates** looks now. Once a version is downloaded, **Install and Restart** here, or **Install update** in the title bar, stops the backend and restarts the app on the new version.
- **Usage statistics**: **Count feature usage (beta)** counts, per day, which pages you open and which built-in apps you launch. It is off by default and keeps only the counts, on this computer: never content, file names or conversations.
- **Maintenance**: **Export Diagnostics** saves the app's logs and diagnostic information in a file you can send to support. **Restart Backend** restarts The One's local services; use it when the app tells you a change needs it.

### Appearance

First choose **Follow the system**, **Light** or **Dark**; Light is the default. Light uses **Plain White**, black on white with no tint, and Dark uses **Graphite**, a neutral dark grey.

Then, under **Color schemes**, pick a scheme for light and one for dark. Besides Plain White and Graphite there are 11 ready-made schemes: **Xuan Paper**, **Moon White**, **Celadon**, **Lotus Mauve** and **Frost** for light, and **Ink Night**, **Deep Indigo**, **Pine Soot**, **Cinnabar Lacquer**, **Lilac** and **Misty Rain** for dark. Xuan Paper and Ink Night are the former defaults.

For a scheme of your own, choose **Customize** on a ready-made one, or **New from Plain White** under the list (**New from Graphite** on the dark side); **Edit** changes one you made. A scheme has seven base colors, from which the others follow: the surface, the text, the accent, and the colors that mark what needs you, what is running, what was delivered and what is failing. A WCAG AA contrast check runs as you go, and a scheme that misses it still saves. **Copy scheme JSON** and **Import…** in the editor take a scheme to another computer.

## Models and sign-in

### Model connections

The connections Agents send their model requests through: the official connection that comes with a The One membership, Sign in with ChatGPT, API keys for OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Gemini, and custom providers, including models on your own computer. See [Model connections](./api-configuration).

### Default model

A **model preset** keeps a model, the connection it goes through and its reasoning together; Agents given no model of their own use the one marked **Global default**. See [Models](../capabilities/models).

## Runs by itself

This section is the **Background & triggers** page: see, change, try and add the triggers that wake your Agents, and the switches that keep The One running in the background after you close the window. See [Triggers and running in the background](../triggers).

## Phone and other programs

### Phone channel

Give your main assistant work from your phone, answer the questions Agents ask and see what they deliver, through your private Agent Room room. See [Phone channel and outside callers](../remote).

### Outside callers

Let Claude Code, Codex, agents that speak A2A or your own scripts on this computer use your teams as a tool, each with a token of its own. A program with a token can have your teams do anything they can do, so give tokens only to programs you trust. See [Phone channel and outside callers](../remote).

## Data and undo

### Action log

Every tool call an Agent makes is recorded here, and files Agents changed or deleted can be put back while their copies are kept; the log only records and undoes, it never stops anything. See [Action log, undo and stopping](../action-log).

### Storage

This page shows how much disk the app's data takes, item by item (the database, command output, tool results, logs, the action log and its trash, evidence packs and sent requests), and how long each is kept. When free space runs low, the title bar warns you and working Agents are told. Lower still, protection mode stops saving full command output and database snapshots and removes expired files at once; Agents keep running.

**Plugin workspaces** hold what Plugins download, render and keep in their browsers; **Clear** deletes a workspace for good. The page also counts the Docker data and the git worktrees that Agents leave outside the app, but never deletes them: it gives you the command to run in a terminal yourself.

### Memory recall

Choose the **Embedding model** that ranks memories, project knowledge and the conclusions of earlier sessions by meaning, or rebuild the index. See [Memory and recall](../capabilities/memory).

## Developer

### Developer view

The **Developer view** page's switch, **Show what developers look at**, is off by default. Turned on, it draws the same data the way a developer looks at it: the main assistant's conversation shows its system prompt, the notices as written for its model, and every tool call with its arguments and result; matter pages, the **Needs you** page and the cards of what waits for you show IDs; and Teams and tools adds Atlas, Definition Spaces, All runs and Evaluations. Only the display changes, not the data. Cmd+Shift+D (Ctrl+Shift+D on Windows) turns it on or off anywhere, and while it is on, the title bar says **Developer view**; a click there turns it off.

Below the switch, **Open all runs** opens All runs: every run in the current Space, the working groups' runs included, down to each Agent's messages, context and tools. The main assistant's own conversation stays on the home page. See [All runs (developer view)](./all-runs).

## The app menu and the tray

### The app menu

The **The One** menu in the macOS menu bar holds **Open Logs** (Cmd+L), **Export Diagnostics**, **Check for Updates**, **Install Downloaded Update**, **Restart Backend**, **Stop All Agents** (Cmd+Shift+.), **Log Out** and **Quit**. Next to it, **View** has **Reload**, **Developer Tools** and zoom, and **Edit** has the usual undo, cut, copy and paste.

On Windows the menu bar is hidden: use the tray menu to stop all Agents, and **Settings › General** for diagnostics, updates and restarting the backend.

### The tray menu

The One's icon sits in the macOS menu bar or the Windows system tray. On macOS it shows how many things wait for you, the same number as beside **Conversation** in the sidebar; on Windows a click on the icon opens the **Needs you** page. The icon's menu holds, from the top:

- How many questions, finished items and failures are still open (**Open:** …, or **No open questions or results**), with up to five of them listed. Click one to open it: a working group's opens the Agent it came from in [All runs](./all-runs), the main assistant's opens the home page.
- When you have triggers, a line saying how many are waiting and when the next one is due, which opens **Background & triggers**, and **Pause All Triggers** (or **Resume All Triggers** while they are paused).
- **Open Needs You**, which opens the **Needs you** page, and **Show The One**, which brings the window to the front.
- **Stop All Agents** and **Quit**.

**Stop All Agents** asks you to confirm, then interrupts every Agent, ends the commands they started, and pauses every run and trigger until you resume them. See [Action log, undo and stopping](../action-log).

### The … menu at the top left

The **…** menu at the top left of the window gives the version, its release date and the update status, and holds **Check Updates** (**Install and Restart** once a new version is downloaded), **Open website**, **Zoom In**, **Zoom Out**, **Reset Zoom** and **Keyboard shortcuts**.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Press **?** anywhere you are not typing, or Cmd+/ (Ctrl+/ on Windows), to see every shortcut; the **…** menu at the top left of the window has **Keyboard shortcuts** too. These work everywhere:

| Keys | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| F6 / Shift+F6 | Move to the next or previous part of the window |
| Cmd+K or Cmd+F (Ctrl+K or Ctrl+F) | Go to this page's search |
| Shift+Esc | Go to the message box |
| Cmd+Shift+A (Ctrl+Shift+A) | Open the quick menu of the sidebar's **Apps**; it is there once a Plugin brings an app you can open, see [Plugins and Apps](../capabilities/plugins) |
| Cmd+Shift+D (Ctrl+Shift+D) | Turn the developer view on or off |
| Esc | Close a dialog, menu or drawer |

On macOS the app menu adds two more: Cmd+L opens the logs and Cmd+Shift+. stops all Agents.

The list also covers the session and preset lists, the message box and answers, the review pane, and the editors and canvases.
